## Scope

Competitively selected into NASA's Mission Concept Academy and chosen by my team as its
Electrical Engineer. The work was proposal development and trade research rather than
hardware build.

## What I researched

Power generation and storage options for a rover operating in a space environment:

- **Perovskite solar cells**, weighed against conventional photovoltaics on efficiency
  and specific power against durability under radiation and thermal cycling.
- **Lithium-sulfur and solid-state batteries**, evaluated primarily on specific energy
  against cycle life and technology maturity.
- **Power management units**, covering how generated power is conditioned, distributed,
  and protected across subsystems.

The recurring theme across all three was that the highest-performing option on paper is
frequently not the correct selection once mission duration, environment, and maturity
enter the trade. That framing has carried directly into how I approach component
selection on propulsion hardware.

## Why it is here

This project is the reason I am comfortable making a selection argument in writing and
defending it. It sits at the research and systems end of my experience rather than the
hands-on end, and it was where I learned to structure a trade study before reaching for
an answer.
